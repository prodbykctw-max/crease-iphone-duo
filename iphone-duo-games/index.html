Games for the iPhone Duo: what actually uses the fold

Apple's first foldable goes on sale Friday, October 23, 2026, with pre-orders from October 16. Almost everything you install on it on day one will treat the inner screen as nothing more than a bigger rectangle. A game built for the fold does something different: it treats the hinge as part of the game.

By prodbyKCTW (Melvin D. Brown III) · Published October 5, 2026 · Device details below are Apple's own published specifications

The difference between a responsive game and a fold-native one

A responsive game detects the larger screen and scales up. Buttons get bigger, the playfield gets wider, the layout reflows. That is a layout change, and every well-built app already does it — it is the same work a site does between a phone and a tablet.

A fold-native game treats the crease as a physical feature with meaning. The hinge is not an obstacle to route around; it is a boundary, a net, a border, a centre line. Unfolding the device does not just reveal more pixels — it changes what the game is.

The test is simple: if you could get the same experience on a non-folding phone with a bigger screen, the game is responsive, not fold-native. Nearly everything shipping on October 23 will fail that test, because the install base is one device that does not exist yet.

Four things the hinge is genuinely good for

A shared boundary between two people
Unfolded and laid flat, the Duo gives each player their own half with a physical line between them. That is a tabletop. Air hockey, pong, foosball, tug-of-war, Battleship, anything where the whole point is that the two sides are opposed.
Two surfaces that mean different things
Half-folded, the device stands up by itself with one plane facing each way. One half becomes a board and the other becomes a hand of cards, a control panel, or a second player's private view.
A hidden-information line
A crease you cannot see past is a screen in the literal sense. Any game that depends on one player not seeing the other's move has, for the first time, a single-device way to do it.
Folding as an input
Closing and opening the device is a gesture the game can read. It can mean pause, swap, flip the board, or change the rules — and it costs the player nothing to learn, because they already know how to fold their phone.

What CREASE does with it

CREASE is a free neon air hockey game that puts the centre line of the table on the hinge. It runs in any browser, with no download and no account, and it already works today on whatever phone you are holding.

Folded, the outer screen is a pocket table — one player against the CPU, the crease running across the middle. Opened, the inner screen becomes a full table with a player on each side of the hinge, each defending their own goal. Fold or unfold in the middle of a match and the score, the power-ups and the puck come with you — the game changes shape without stopping.

How it plays

  • First to 7. Drag your paddle; the puck does the rest.
  • SMASH — flick your paddle hard into the puck to send it in fast.
  • BUMP — bounce the puck off the gold bumpers sitting on the crease. Score off one and it counts as a bank shot.
  • Solo, pass-and-play, or online. Play the CPU on easy, normal or hard; hand the phone over for two players on one screen; or open online play and tap quick match to be paired with another person, or share a room code to play someone specific.
  • Also in VR. There is a Meta Quest version with hand tracking, in the headset browser.

Common questions

What games are there for the iPhone Duo?

Very few that are built for the fold specifically, because the device ships October 23, 2026 and until then nobody can test on real hardware. Most launch-day games will be existing titles that scale up to the inner display. CREASE is a free browser game built fold-first, playable now on any phone.

Do I need to wait for the iPhone Duo to play CREASE?

No. It runs in any modern browser on any phone, tablet or computer today. On the Duo it uses the hinge as the centre line of the table; everywhere else it uses the middle of the screen.

Is CREASE free?

Yes — free, no download, no account, no payment.

Can two people play on one phone?

Yes. Turn the phone to landscape and each player controls the paddle on their half of the screen. On the Duo, unfolded, that half is a physical half.

Can I install it like an app?

Yes. In Safari, tap Share then "Add to Home Screen"; in Chrome, use "Install app" or "Add to Home screen". It then opens full-screen with its own icon and works the same way.

Can I play other people online?

Yes. Open online play and tap quick match to be paired with whoever else is looking for a game, or send a friend a room code to play them directly. It is free and needs no account.
